## Question blueprint

| Block | Quick | In depth | Facets | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| House | 6 | 10 | courage vs. caution, ambition, loyalty, curiosity/learning, rule-breaking, what you'd want to be remembered for, how you treat outsiders, dilemmas between friends and principles | Single-choice (trade-offs); one self-description item may be multi-select |
| Wand | 4 | 8 | temperament under pressure, adaptability (→ flexibility), conviction (→ core), affinities: nature, craft, healing, duelling, intellect (→ wood), presence/size of personality (→ length) | Single-choice; affinities may be multi-select |
| Patronus | 3 | 6 | kinds of happy memories, instinct when cornered, solitary vs. pack, day vs. night, environment you're drawn to | Happy memories: multi-select; the rest single-choice |
| Ilvermorny | 1 | 2 | which quality you'd most want valued in you (see framing below) | Single-choice |
| Extras | - | 2 | fears, comforting smells, ideal job | Fears and smells: multi-select; job: single-choice |

4 options per item for single-choice; multi-selects may use 4–6 options (cap: half, rounded down). Each option maps to a house (or wand trait / patronus family / Ilvermorny house). Mix plain preference items with in-world scenarios.

**Ilvermorny framing**: the houses are traditionally mind, body, soul and heart, but those words overlap ("the brain is part of the body") and "soul" depends on belief. Never offer them as options. Use non-overlapping, belief-neutral qualities instead, e.g. *reasoning and knowledge* (Horned Serpent, scholars), *courage in action* (Wampus, warriors), *imagination and a sense of adventure* (Thunderbird, adventurers), *care for others* (Pukwudgie, healers). Explain the traditional labels only in the results.

Example:

> **5.** You find an unfinished, brilliant spell in a forbidden book. You…
> A) Finish it yourself: tonight (Ravenclaw/Gryffindor)
> B) Bring it to a teacher you trust (Hufflepuff)
> C) Keep it; it could be very useful one day (Slytherin)
> D) Show your friends and try it together (Gryffindor/Hufflepuff)

## Wand lore pointers

Use canonical wood meanings (e.g. holly: quests/temper; vine: hidden depths; yew: power over life and death; hawthorn: contradictions; cedar: loyalty and perceptiveness; walnut: intelligence/innovation; ash: stubborn single-mindedness; elder: rare destiny…) and core traits (phoenix: rare, independent, choosy; dragon: powerful, quick to learn, temperamental; unicorn: consistent, loyal, hardest to turn to dark arts). Rigid flexibility ↔ firm convictions; swishy ↔ adaptability.
